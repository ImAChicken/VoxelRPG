- Plants bend from the root (2026-11-21, Eddie: "the bottom of the sprite should be anchored and the higher up the more it is affected"): every corner of a plant's picture leans by its height above the plant's root, more than in proportion (Mesher.h sprites: pad0 bits 4-7, counting other plant blocks under a stack; shaders/chunk.vert `bend`). A flower head on its stem now leans with the stem's top instead of staying put at the joint, and tall plants' tips move most. (Settings > Plants: Windy.)
- Portal views in the far world's light (2026-11-21, Eddie: "main world is not showing right time of day from dungeon"): looking through a gate into another world, the view takes that world's time of day (sky, sun or moon, haze, the light on its voxels and bodies) and its land's sun and shade (the map of its distant terrain, kept while you're away), not the world you're standing in. Script commands `pad <buttons>` (the pretend controller, VOXELRPG_FAKE_PAD), `newworld <name> <seed>`; `dbg` shows combat, the gate in reach and its menu.
- Light from the land (2026-11-21, Eddie: "lighting normals based on time of day using our terrain map"): a coarse height map of the whole world (8 voxels a cell or more, at most 1024 a side) built from the generator on a thread of its own (`render/TerrainLight.h`, owned by the world's `FarTerrain`). Settings > Graphics, each with a plain setting:
  - Sun shading (Terrain, default): the ground's tops (and plants) near the ground the map knows, and the distant terrain, are lit by the land's smooth slope toward the sun or moon, so hillsides brighten and darken through the day.
  - Terrain shadows (On, default): per cell the height below which the light is behind a hill, from one sweep across the map toward the light (worked out again on the thread each time the light turns 0.35°). Valleys fall into shadow at dawn and dusk; the voxels, props, smooth roofs, the distant terrain and characters (their sky light) all take it. Coarse by design: hills shade, houses don't.
  - Cloud shadows (Off, default): drifting patches with the wind. There are no clouds drawn in the sky yet.
  - Shaders can `#include "file"` now (`Shader::readFile`); the shared part is `shaders/terrainlight.glsl`. Portal views of another world are lit plain. Script command `cmd <console command>`.
- Rendering optimizations, second pass (2026-11-21):
  - Hidden chunk culling (Settings > Graphics, on by default): after the world pass each section in view is tested as a box against the depth (an occlusion query, nothing drawn); one that didn't show isn't drawn until a later test says it shows again. Answers are read a frame or two late, never waited for; the section round the camera, and everything after a jump or a change of world, always draws (`OpenGLRenderer::occludedSection / queueOcclusion / runOcclusion`). Inside a house: 25 sections drawn instead of 72, 0.3 M vertices instead of 17.6 M, same picture. F3 and `dbg` show the hidden count.
  - Render scale (Settings > Graphics: Full, Auto, 85%, 70%, 50%): the 3D view drawn into an offscreen buffer at that share of the window each way and stretched over it; the HUD stays sharp. Auto times the scene on the GPU and steps the scale between 50% and full to stay inside a 60 fps frame (`OpenGLRenderer::beginScene / endScene / steer`; main.cpp's `drawWorld` wraps the scene, `drawWorldScene`).
  - The distant terrain takes each biome's plants and trees into its colour (`FarTerrain::makeLook`, from decorations.toml): grass shows its flowers and tufts as much as they cover it, forest its trees' own leaves by their shares (red oak, maples, birch; the rest the built-in oak), so it matches the voxel chunks where they begin.
  - A world nobody is in and nothing sees (single player: not through a portal) steps half as often, two ticks at a time (`Worlds::SLOW_STEPS`); its NPCs keep the same pace. Servers step every world fully.
  - Portal views draw their sections near to far too.

- No flash crossing a server's gate (2026-11-21, Eddie: "a flash of the previous world… it needs to be seamless"): the game crosses the moment its own simulation does, as in single player, instead of waiting for the server's word (between the two, the camera was behind the opening with nothing drawn through it). The client's copy of a world now has the server's links to cross (`GameSession::attachRemote` gives the simulation its portals); `remoteTick` takes the player's crossing and does the swap (`crossIntoWatched`, shared with the server-first case). The server, with the same inputs, crosses the same way; its Swap then arrives on the watch connection as confirmation (`pendingCross`; none in 3 s: join again where it has you). Inputs sent on the new connection before the server swaps are kept and used (a watcher's Input isn't dropped; `promote` keeps them), so there's no stop either.
- No pop-in crossing a server's gate (2026-11-21, Eddie: "brutal pop-in"): the far world is streamed whole round its gate while you're near it (full mesh distance, all workers; was 3 chunks), the world you left stays whole too, and the swap moves the camera with you at once and reads the new world's realm at once (the frame after a swap used to update the streamers from the old camera and with no links, unloading both). Each world's distant terrain and what's been seen of it are kept across crossings (`farKept`), not made again. Script `dbg` lists unmeshed chunks near you and the far streamers.
